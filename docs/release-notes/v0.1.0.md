# Earwitness 0.1.0

The first release of Earwitness: an open-source proof of concept that estimates where a
recording was made from background thunder and propeller-aircraft sound, matched against
public lightning (NOAA GOES GLM) and flight (adsb.lol) records. Its purpose is privacy
awareness: check your own recordings for location clues, and see what does and does not
remove them.

Install: [docs/INSTALL.md](https://github.com/cajdata/earwitness/blob/v0.1.0/docs/INSTALL.md) (Windows build, or `pipx install earwitness` on
Windows and Linux). Check the install with `earwitness demo`.

## What is in it

- **Audit** (`earwitness audit`, and the app's first page): reports which location clues a
  recording contains (thunder, propeller flyovers, mains hum, metadata tags) and what helps
  against each.
- **Thunder method** (`earwitness analyze thunder`): matches the recording's low-frequency
  loudness against GOES GLM lightning flashes over a date and region.
- **Aircraft method** (`earwitness analyze aircraft`): matches Doppler curves measured in the
  app's spectrogram tools against adsb.lol flight tracks, with two flyovers, or one flyover and
  a known start time.
- **Desktop app** (`earwitness-ui`, or `earwitness ui`): audit, setup, audio tools, run and
  results with a heat map, all local, on a random localhost port guarded by a session token.
- **Simulator** (`earwitness simulate`) for synthetic test recordings with known answers, and
  **`earwitness demo`**, the install check.
- **Windows build**: a zip with `earwitness.exe` and `earwitness-ui.exe`; no Python needed.
- Documentation: [method](https://github.com/cajdata/earwitness/blob/v0.1.0/docs/METHOD.md), [data sources](https://github.com/cajdata/earwitness/blob/v0.1.0/docs/DATA_SOURCES.md),
  [countermeasures](https://github.com/cajdata/earwitness/blob/v0.1.0/docs/COUNTERMEASURES.md), [validation](https://github.com/cajdata/earwitness/blob/v0.1.0/docs/VALIDATION.md), and the site,
  [earwitness.dev](https://earwitness.dev).

## How well it works

From [docs/VALIDATION.md](https://github.com/cajdata/earwitness/blob/v0.1.0/docs/VALIDATION.md), with every failure included:

- **Synthetic acceptance.** Thunder: 9 of 10 storm cases located first within 5 km (10 of 10
  with the Pearson score), and a continental-US day searched in 114 s. Aircraft: 10 of 10
  two-flyover cases and 10 of 10 one-flyover-with-clock cases located first within 200 m.
- **Real aircraft recordings: 2 of 2 located within about 200 m inside a metro-sized region.**
  colsto's published test recording: 173 m off, ranked first, in 9 minutes with 12 scoring
  processes. A 21-minute phone video: 190 m off with the phone's own clock and both flyovers;
  a 150 s excerpt was enough.
- **Real thunder recordings: 1 of 10 located.** An 8-minute clip with a clap 0.6 km away on
  a day of rare lightning was located 2.2 km off. Nine failed: two in dense storms, five with
  only distant rumble or a busy day of lightning in the region, and two security-camera clips,
  a single clap with no clock and a clocked clip of five claps that ranked the truth sixth,
  4.3 km off.

## Known limitations

- **Thunder fails in dense storms and on distant rumble.** At 20 or more flashes a minute the
  claps blur into one rolling sound that fits many places and times, and thunder 10 to 25 km
  away gives only broad bumps that rain, traffic and streams imitate. A thunder recording
  needs claps within about 10 km and rare lightning in the region and day searched, or a
  known recording time. A single clap with no clock fits almost anywhere.
- **One flyover of a common trainer is ambiguous**, at any clip length, even with a known
  time: other trainers flying at pattern speed nearby leave nearly the same curve. A
  distinctive aircraft with a known time needed about 90 s around its flyover.
- **A two-flyover search without a clock failed in a training-dense metro area** (the true
  pair ranked hundreds deep). With the phone's clock, the same recording was located.
- **Aircraft searches need a region.** A continental two-flyover search is beyond v0.1 on one
  machine. A metro-sized region takes minutes; a known start time cuts it to about a minute.
- **GLM covers the Americas only**, from December 2017 (GOES-East) and February 2019
  (GOES-West). It is optical: it misses some flashes, includes cloud-to-cloud ones, places
  each flash to about 3 to 4 km, and saw no flash in a winter thundersnow case.
- **adsb.lol publishes each day at about 03:30 UTC the next day**, so a recording can't be
  matched against flights until then. Small aircraft are not always seen by ADS-B receivers,
  especially low and far from cities.
- **Disk and memory.** One day of adsb.lol data is about 4 GB to download, and a regional
  aircraft search can use tens of GB of memory for its flight-track cache. The cache folder
  grows with every day and region analysed; delete it when done.
- **Measurement is manual.** Doppler curves are traced and fitted by hand in the app;
  there is no automatic flyover detection. The audit's detectors are heuristics tuned on
  synthetic recordings, not validated classifiers.
- **Not covered:** helicopters and jets, lightning outside the Americas (no MET Norway or
  other adapter yet), and other clues such as mains hum (see
  [FUTURE_SOURCES.md](https://github.com/cajdata/earwitness/blob/v0.1.0/docs/FUTURE_SOURCES.md)).
- **Run times** depend on the region, dates and clues; [VALIDATION.md](https://github.com/cajdata/earwitness/blob/v0.1.0/docs/VALIDATION.md)
  lists each run's time. A thunder search takes seconds to two minutes. An aircraft search
  takes about a minute with a known time, and 9 to 90 minutes for a metro region without one,
  depending on the scoring processes.
- **App limits:** a run can't be cancelled except by closing the app; one run at a time; the
  app always needs a region (a whole-world search is command-line only).
- **Platforms:** the Windows build is not code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn. The build was
  tested on Windows 11. On Linux the desktop window needs a Qt or GTK toolkit, and without one
  the app opens in the browser. The default map tiles come from OpenStreetMap, whose usage
  policy rules out heavy use; set another tile server in Setup if you need one.

## Responsible use

Use Earwitness on your own recordings or synthetic ones. The project publishes no results that
locate other people's recordings, runs no hosted service, downloads no media from URLs, and
bundles no third-party data. Blitzortung and LightningMaps data are never used.

The method is colsto's, reconstructed from his three videos; his code is not public.
