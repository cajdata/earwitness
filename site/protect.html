Protect your recordings

What gives a recording away is the timing between its sounds and anything that says when it was made. Change those, and the search has much less to work with. Filters and compression don't help.

First, check what's in it

earwitness audit my-video.mp4

Or open the desktop app, which starts with the same audit. It lists the clues it hears and what helps against each.

What helps

StepWhy it helps
helps Cut the recording A cut changes the gap between sounds. A search for two passing planes needs the real gap: after one 60 second cut between them, it found nothing at all. Several cuts also throw off thunder, moving the answer in our test from 0.2 km to 4.5 km.
helps Remove the clock A known start time is the biggest help to a search. With the phone's own clock, a phone video was located to 190 m. Without it, the same search failed. Hide on-screen clocks and timestamps, and don't say the time out loud.
helps Strip the metadata Phone videos often store GPS coordinates and the time they were made. That gives the place away without any sound analysis. Many apps can remove it. With ffmpeg: ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -map_metadata -1 -c copy out.mp4
helps Heavy noise reduction Strong noise reduction can wipe out faint plane sounds and quieter claps. colsto's tests showed this; we haven't measured it ourselves.

What doesn't help

StepWhat happened
doesn't help Low-cut or bass filter The plane's curve came back as soon as the volume was turned up, in colsto's tests and ours. Even a steep filter that removed the main tone left its overtones, and the search still found the spot.
doesn't help Compression (MP3, AAC, phone calls) Low-quality MP3 and AAC changed nothing in our tests, and the curve survived phone-call compression in colsto's. Codecs keep loud, steady tones, and a propeller's drone is one.
doesn't help Talking over it The curve was still there underneath, in colsto's tests.
not enough Keeping it short Shorter clips are harder, but short is not safe. With a clock, a 150 second phone clip with two planes in it was located to 130 m. About 90 seconds around one distinctive plane was enough, and in colsto's test a 1 minute clip was solved. An 8 minute clip with one close thunderclap was located to 2.2 km.

When a clue is weak anyway

  • Thunder in a big storm, where claps run together, or thunder far away (10 to 25 km) was not located.
  • A single clap with no clock fits almost anywhere.
  • One flyover of a common training plane was ambiguous, even with a clock: other trainers nearby make nearly the same sound.

These are measured results, not guarantees. A recording that defeats one search may still give itself away to a better one, or to a different clue entirely.

Not tested yet

Changing the pitch or speed of the audio, or replacing the soundtrack with music, would likely help, but nobody has measured them here.
